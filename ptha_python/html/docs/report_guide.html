Reading report.html

Step 9 writes report.html into every zone folder. It is one self-contained page: the numbers come from the files the earlier steps wrote, and the charts and figures are drawn or embedded in the page, so it opens offline. Three things load from the internet when the page opens (web fonts, and for the mesh-on-a-map card the Leaflet library and Esri tiles); without a connection the fonts fall back and that map is empty. This page lists every section the report has, in the order it has them, says what each shows, and marks which ones appear only with certain options.

Step 9 takes no flags: python <folder>/steps/step9_report.py. It stops with missing <path> and Run step6_write_input.py first. or Run step7_run.py first. when its inputs are absent. Everything else (steps 7b, 7c, 8) is optional and only changes which cards and tables appear. Re-run it after any change to an earlier step.

The top of the page: switch, header and banner

Three things come before any number. Read them first.

BlockWhat it says
View switch (segmented runs only)two tabs, "Segmented" (report.html, the zone's answer: 0.5 times the unsegmented branch plus 0.5 times the segments) and "Unsegmented only" (report_unsegmented.html, the zone as one fault). A segmented run writes both pages; an unsegmented run has one page and no switch.

Step by step

The section "Step by step" has one card per step, in the order below. The cards are read back from the files the steps wrote, so nothing is typed into the page by hand. Cards marked with an option or a step appear only then.

The rupture explorer (in the STEP 7 card)

An interactive picture of LEVEL 2, drawn from this run's own mesh and scenarios. Controls: Magnitude (every Mw of the run), Show (FAUS, VAUS or both; the VAUS choices are disabled when step 7b has not run), a FAUS rupture slider with a play button that steps through every placement, and a VAUS realisation menu that picks one of the VAUS fields drawn from the current FAUS rupture. The map colours the cells of the chosen rupture; a text line gives its block (cells along strike x down dip), its area and how many times the scaling relation's area that is, its slip, its magnitude with the variable rigidity (shown in every run) and, for a VAUS field, its share of its parent's rate or "rate 0" above the peak-slip limit. A side panel, chosen by a Panel menu, plots either the area divided by the scaling relation's median area (with the relation's one-sigma band) or the uniform slip in metres (with PTHA18's limit, 7.5 times the median-area slip, in red) for every FAUS rupture of the magnitude and every VAUS field of the current rupture; hollow dots are fields above the limit.

Mesh with uneven cellsWith --rupture-size local the blocks of one magnitude have different sizes, and the explorer shows each with its own.

The logic-tree levels named on the page

The report names the levels in the STEP 7 card and in the sections below it. They are not sections themselves. Each answers one question.

LevelWhat it decides
LEVEL 0: segmentationwhether the zone is one fault or a union of segments. A segmented run gives the unsegmented branch and the union of segments 0.5 each. See the section "LEVEL 0: segmentation".
LEVEL 1: parametersthe branches of the tree: the coupling, the slip rate (the convergence times the coupling, divided by the cosine of the dip), the b value, the largest magnitude and the Gutenberg-Richter type.
LEVEL 2: rupture generationthe uniform-slip ruptures of each magnitude placed on the mesh, and the Gutenberg-Richter a value of each branch, set by moment balance.
LEVEL 3: observed earthquakeshow the GCMT earthquakes of the zone move the weight of each branch. The card gives the total variation distance between the prior and the posterior weights, from 0 (no change) to 1 (a complete reshuffle).
LEVEL 4: edge correctionthe probability of ruptures that touch the ends of the zone, which is fitted so that the slip released along the zone matches the convergence. See "What the moment-balance target is".
LEVEL 5: percentilesthe percentile band of the branches (the page draws the 16th to 84th) and, on a segmented run, how the segments are combined.

The exceedance-rate curve

The curve answers one question: how often, per year, does an earthquake of at least a given magnitude happen on this interface?

Part of the chartWhat it is
Horizontal axismoment magnitude Mw, from 7.2 to 9.6.
Vertical axisevents per year, on a logarithmic scale from 0.0001 to 1. Each gridline is a factor of ten.
Solid linethis run: the mean over all branches of the logic tree. On a segmented run it is the unsegmented branch.
Shaded bandthe 16th to 84th percentile across all branches. It shows the uncertainty of the logic tree itself, not measurement noise. On the segmented page it is the whole zone's band (the mix); on the unsegmented page it is the unsegmented branch's.
Dashed linethe official PTHA18 curve. It appears only when step 8 has run. For a segmented zone it is PTHA18's unsegmented branch.
Segmented page onlyone fine dotted line per segment, a dashed line for the union of segments, and a thick dotted line for the source zone (the 0.5 / 0.5 mix), which is the zone's answer and belongs with the band.

Each series can be hidden with the buttons under the chart. Hovering over the chart shows the magnitude and the rate of the nearest point of each series. Three tiles give the rate of the unsegmented branch at Mw 7.2, 8.0 and 9.0. The curve stops where the rate reaches 0, and the band's lower edge sits on the bottom of the chart where the 16th percentile is 0.

Reading a return periodA rate of 0.01 per year is one earthquake of that size or larger every 100 years on average. It is not a schedule. Over a long time, the number of events varies a lot around the average.

LEVEL 0: segmentation (segmented runs only)

This section follows the exceedance-rate curve on the segmented page. It has these parts, in order:

  • How the zone is divided: one bar per model on a common 0-100% scale (this run, and PTHA18's own segmentation after step 8), each colour a segment, and a map of the mesh cells coloured by segment (PTHA18's mesh beside this run's after step 8).
  • A callout on where this run's boundaries came from: Berryman et al. (2015) Table 3.1 (the default) or Bird (2003)'s plate pairs (--segment-boundaries bird).
  • A segment table: plate pair, columns, convergence, coupling min/pref/max, mw_max_observed, number of GCMT events and the rate at Mw 7.2 of each segment.
  • After step 8 only: PTHA18's own segments (the same columns for PTHA18's segments) and this run against PTHA18, all three curves (unsegmented, union and mix at six magnitudes). Without step 8 a note says there is no official segmented run to compare with.
  • The segmented curves: a chart of each segment, the union and the mix (and PTHA18's, after step 8).
  • What segmenting changed in this run: a table at Mw 7.2, 8.0, 8.8 and 9.2 with the unsegmented rate, the union, the difference and the mix.

The union usually lies above the unsegmented curve for small magnitudes, because several smaller faults produce more moderate earthquakes than one large fault. It lies below it for the largest magnitudes, because no single segment is long enough to host a rupture across the whole zone (in the kermadectonga2 example the crossing is at Mw 8.44). That crossing is the effect of segmentation. The last column of the table is the number the run reports for the zone. On an unsegmented run there is no such section: the banner at the top says the run is unsegmented and how to make it segmented.

Prior and posterior weights (LEVEL 3)

This section shows the engine's own figure fig_branch_fan_<zone>.png: every branch of the logic tree drawn twice, with the weights before the observed earthquakes are used (left) and after (right), against the observed events (green). Each branch is one curve. If the posterior curves are close to the prior ones, the earthquakes did not change the answer much. Two tiles give the total variation distance and the number of observed events. After step 8, the same figure of the official run is shown below it. On a segmented run this section describes the unsegmented branch.

What the moment-balance target is (LEVEL 4)

Added up over every scenario, the slip the earthquakes release along the zone should have the same shape as the convergence along the trench. That shape is the target of LEVEL 4 (Bird's convergent rate of each column, or a flat line if the convergence is a constant). Near the ends of the zone fewer scenarios overlap, so the released slip sags there.

LEVEL 4 fits one multiplier for the probability of scenarios that touch an end, until the two shapes agree as closely as possible. The chart (interactive, drawn from integrated_slip_<zone>.csv) has three curves: no edge correction, the target, and the edge-corrected result, scaled to the same total because only their shapes are compared. The text gives the multiplier of this run and, when step 8 has run, the one of the official run.

Exceedance rate by magnitude, full table

The table gives the mean exceedance rate, in events per year, at every magnitude the percentile file has (from 7.2 in steps of 0.1), next to the official run's (and the difference) when step 8 has run; without it the official columns read "n/a". Rows stop where the rate is zero in both runs. On a segmented run both columns describe the whole zone, the LEVEL 0 mix.

Which SLAB product this zone used

This section names the geometry product that step 1 downloads for the zone, quotes PTHA18's rule (zones whose names end in 2 use SLAB2.0; the others use SLAB1.0) and says what it implies for the comparison with PTHA18. It does not appear for an external mesh (--mesh-file).

This run vs. the official PTHA18 run

This section appears only when step 8 has run. It compares this run with PTHA18's own run of the same zone. The two runs use the same engine and the same zone, but different inputs: this run uses public geometry and a fresh GCMT subset, while the official run uses PTHA18's own unit-source table and earthquake catalogue. The table gives mean dip, convergence, edge multiplier, the prior-to-posterior shift and the exceedance rate at Mw 7.2 side by side, with the difference (on a segmented run the Mw 7.2 row is the unsegmented branch). A callout names the input that explains most of the gap, or says the two runs agree when the Mw 7.2 rates differ by less than 1%.

A small difference is what the port should give. A large difference is a question to check, starting with the inputs in the step 6 card. When step 8 did not run, the report is complete for steps 1-7 and says so in the cards that would have used it.

The numbers behind the page

Step 9 writes the CSV files behind the charts and tables to outputs/exports/; the last card of "Step by step" lists them. They are plain CSV, so they can be opened in a spreadsheet or read with a script, and the report is regenerated from the same files.

FileHolds
mesh_cells.csvone row per unit source: its down-dip and along-strike numbers, centroid lon, lat, depth_km, the Bird convergent_slip_mm_per_yr of its column and, on a segmented run, its segment.
posterior_mean_rate_curve.csvthe mean exceedance rate against Mw (a copy of step 7's rate_curves.csv, with the posterior weights).
scenario_rates_source_zone.csvsegmented runs only: every scenario's rate for the whole zone.
convergence.csvthree rows, item,value,units: convergence_horizontal, mean_dip and slip_rate (the convergence divided by the cosine of the mean dip).
convergence_profile.csvone row per along-strike column: alongstrike_number, div_mm_per_yr, convergent_slip_mm_per_yr, distance_to_bird_km and, on a segmented run, segment.
*_official.csv versionsafter step 8: mesh_cells_official.csv, posterior_mean_rate_curve_official.csv, scenario_rates_source_zone_official.csv (segmented), convergence_official.csv and convergence_profile_official.csv (its own column count), same columns, for PTHA18's run.

Other files of the report: the figures it draws are saved in figures/ (scratch_mesh_map.png, scratch_mesh_map_convergence.png, mesh_profile.png, segments_map.png on a segmented run, and the comparison figures after step 8). Step 7c's own files, named *_variable_mu.csv, are in outputs/, not in exports/.
