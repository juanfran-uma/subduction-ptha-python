Geometry: from SLAB to mesh

1. Clean ends: cutting the zone's ends square

A zone ends wherever the SLAB data (or the clip window) stops. That end is often crooked, or the zone narrows to a point there. Step 1 cuts such an end along one straight down-dip line, so that every contour starts and stops on the same line across the zone. A clean end is left alone.

At a glance

Why a crooked end matters

The mesh builder (step 2) pins the first and last column of cells to the contours' own end points: the end column always joins the end of the trench to the end of every deeper contour. If the deep contours stop 50 or 200 km along the zone from where the trench stops, that end column runs along the zone instead of across it, and its cells are slanted slivers, sometimes folded. Their area and dip are then meaningless, and the scenarios that touch them are distorted.

Cutting every contour along the same down-dip line fixes the cause: afterwards all contours start and end on one line across the zone.

Down-dip lines, every 5 km of trench

To measure the zone's width and to have ready-made cut lines, step 1 traces a line of steepest descent of D (the depth below the trench) from every 5 km of trench. Such a line crosses every contour at a right angle. It is followed in 1 km steps, with the gradient smoothed over 10 km, until it reaches the cutoff (a "complete" line; its length is the zone's width there) or leaves the data. The median length of the complete lines is the zone's median width, the number the tip test below uses.

Where in the code: slab_contours.down_dip_transects().

How crooked is an end? A preview of its end cells

"How far from square" is measured on the cells the mesh builder would actually make there. From the contours alone, step 1 rebuilds the two columns nearest the end: the end column (the contours' end points) and the next one (the points 50 km in along each contour, one cell length), and splits both into the same number of rows the width rule would give at 50 km (at least 2). The worst corner of those cells, in degrees away from 90, is the end's deviation.

Where in the code: slab_contours._end_deviation().

The rule, exactly

  1. Pointed end? If the zone's width at the outermost complete line (median over 25 km of trench) is below 50% of the median width, cut at the first line where the width is back to 70%.
  2. Otherwise, crooked end? If its deviation is above 45 deg (MAX_END_DEVIATION_DEG), cut along the outermost complete line: the one from the trench's last point if it reaches the cutoff, else the last one that does inside the data.
  3. Otherwise keep the end exactly as SLAB gives it.
  4. Check the cut. The new end is measured again. If it is still more than 45 deg off square, the cut moves inward, one line (5 km) at a time, up to 150 km, stopping at the first position within 45 deg; if none is, the position with the smallest deviation is used. This step is skipped for given end points.
  5. Apply it to every contour: each is cut where it crosses the chosen line, and the trench is shortened to the line's start point.
Where the numbers come fromThe 45 deg limit and the 50% and 70% widths were set by comparing with PTHA18's own hand-made ends. They are constants in the code and can be changed (below).

Where in the code: slab_contours._end_cuts() (taper_tip=0.5, taper_keep=0.7, smooth_km=25) and trim_ends(). Called by below_trench_contours() with clean_ends=True (the default), only when every level was found and none is closed.

Zones with given end points

For the zones with Berryman et al. (2015) end points (step 1), the automatic rule does not run. Both ends are cut at the two given points, whatever their shape, and the cut is not moved. On calabria2, for example, the start is cut along the down-dip line that passes 40 km from its given point, and the end along the line that passes 1 km from its point.

The step 1 log prints, for each end, one of three lines, and the same sentence is the reason of that end in data/step1_contours_info.json (key ends):

      start: CUT, the zone narrows to a point there (38% of its median width at the tip): cut where it is back to 70%; trench shortened by 180 km, deeper contours by up to 243 km; the new end is 9 deg off square
      end  : CUT, its natural end is 72 deg off square: cut along the down-dip line from the trench's last point; trench shortened by 0 km, deeper contours by up to 51 km; the new end is 45 deg off square
      start: kept as it is (17 deg off square, within PTHA18's own range)

The first two lines are from the kermadectonga2 run, the third from puysegur2. The line before them gives the median width, clean ends (an artefact end is cut along a down-dip line; median width 140 km):, and the line after them the trench length after the cuts. A crooked end whose cut line does not start at the trench's last point reads cut along the last down-dip line that reaches the cutoff inside the SLAB data. For given points the heading is zone ends fixed by the given end points (trench_ends):. If a cut cannot be applied, the log says clean ends: NOT applied (reason) and the contours stay uncut; if no down-dip line reaches the cutoff, nothing is printed and ends is null.

Changing or switching off the rule

  • More or fewer cuts: change MAX_END_DEVIATION_DEG (45.0) in lib/slab_contours.py, next to the clean-ends code. It applies to every run of step 1 from then on, for every zone; re-run the zone from step 1.
  • Pointed ends: taper_tip (0.5) and taper_keep (0.7) are default arguments of _end_cuts() in the same file; change the defaults there.
  • No cuts at all: add clean_ends=False to the below_trench_contours(...) call in the zone's steps/step1_fetch_slab2.py. The raw ends are always kept in info["contours_untrimmed"] and drawn dotted in the step 1 figure. For the zones with Berryman end points, those points are always used instead.
  • What it never does: extend a zone. Where PTHA18 extended a zone beyond the SLAB data by hand, this package does not invent geometry: the cut only removes.
