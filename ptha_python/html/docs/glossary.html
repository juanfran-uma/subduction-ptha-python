Glossary

Every technical term used in this guide, in plain words, with the page that explains it. Terms are in alphabetical order; the letters above jump to each group.

ABCDEFGHLMNOPQRSTUV

A

along strike
Parallel to the trench. The columns of the mesh lie side by side along strike, and the rows run down dip. See The mesh.
area-weighted mean
An average in which each cell counts in proportion to its area. The zone's convergence is an area-weighted mean. See Plate convergence.
auto-clip
A flag of step_total.py and of step 1. It cuts the along-strike columns that step 3 found sitting on a different plate boundary, using a window derived from the mesh itself. See Recipes.

B

b-value
The slope of the Gutenberg-Richter law. Each unit of magnitude is 10 to the power b times rarer. It is taken from Berryman et al. (2015) and is uncertain, so the logic tree has a minimum, a preferred and a maximum value. See Logic tree and rates.
Bayesian update
Re-weighting of the logic-tree branches by how well each one predicts the earthquakes observed in the GCMT catalogue (level 3). The rate curves of the branches do not change; only their weights do. See Logic tree and rates.
Berryman et al. (2015)
The study of subduction interfaces that PTHA18 used for its literature values: the seismogenic cutoff, coupling, b-value, largest observed magnitude, and the trench end points of its Table 3.1, which give the segments. The values are read through lib/berryman_params.py. See The input file.
Bird (2003)
A global digital model of the plate boundaries (PB2002). It gives the relative velocity of the plates along each short segment of a boundary, and it is the default source of the convergence rates. See Plate convergence.
bird-griffin
The value of --convergence that reads the plate table PTHA18 used: Bird's steps plus Jonathan Griffin's source-zone traces with their own plate rates. The default bird reads Bird's public catalogue only. See Bird or Bird + Griffin, per zone.
bow-tie cell
A folded cell whose four sides cross, like the two triangles of a bow tie. It has no sensible area or dip. The mesh checks count such cells. See The mesh.
branch
One complete combination of the uncertain inputs (coupling, b-value, maximum magnitude and the shape of the Gutenberg-Richter law). Each branch is one possible model, with a weight. A standard zone has 32,000 of them. See Logic tree and rates.

C

centroid (of an earthquake)
The average location of the slip of an earthquake, as given by its moment tensor. It can differ from the hypocentre by tens of kilometres. See Earthquake catalogue.
clean ends
The rule of step 1 that cuts the end of a zone square, along a down-dip line, when the end would give cells more than 45 degrees off square, or when the zone narrows to a point. See Clean ends.
conditional probability
How the rate of one magnitude is shared between the scenarios of that magnitude. It is the probability that a given scenario ruptures, once an earthquake of that magnitude occurs. See Logic tree and rates.
contour
A line joining the points of the plate interface that have the same depth. The package draws one every 5 km, measured as depth below the nearby trench. See Contours from SLAB.
convergence
How fast the two plates approach each other, horizontally, in millimetres per year. It comes from the plate table chosen by --convergence. See Plate convergence.
copula (comonotonic)
The way the uncertainties of several segments are combined in level 5. It is comonotonic: the segments are taken to be high or low together. It has an effect only on a segmented run (--segmented true). See Logic tree and rates.
coupling
The fraction of the plate motion that is released in earthquakes; the rest slides without an earthquake. It is uncertain, and its minimum, preferred and maximum values come from Berryman et al. (2015). See Logic tree and rates.

D

DART
Deep-ocean tsunami buoys. PTHA18 fitted the weights that share the rate of a scenario among its heterogeneous-slip and variable-area fields to buoy records, and the package uses those weights. See HS and VAUS slip.
dip
The angle of the plate interface below the horizontal: 0 is flat and 90 is vertical. The mean dip of a zone is angle-weighted. See The mesh.
discretizer
The method that cuts the contours into cells: optimal (the default), lm or mid. See optimal, lm and mid.
down dip
Down the slope of the plate interface, away from the trench. The rows of the mesh are stacked down dip. See The mesh.
down-dip line
A line from the trench to the deepest contour that separates two columns of cells. rptha places these lines so that each crosses every contour at a right angle. See The mesh.

E

edge correction (level 4)
A multiplier on the probability of the scenarios that touch an along-strike end of the zone. It is fitted so that the slip released along the zone follows the plate convergence. A segment inherits the zone's correction. See Logic tree and rates.
Esri, Leaflet
Esri provides the map tiles (sea floor and satellite images) of the report's map. Leaflet is the JavaScript library that shows them. Both load from the internet when the report is opened. See Reading report.html.
exceedance rate
How many earthquakes of at least a given magnitude are expected per year. A rate of 0.01 per year means one such earthquake every hundred years on average. See Logic tree and rates.

F

FAUS
Fixed Area Uniform Slip: a scenario is a rectangle of cells, sized by the scaling relation, with one slip value spread uniformly over it. All the rates are computed on FAUS scenarios. See Logic tree and rates.
fingerprint (of a saved mesh)
The settings a mesh was built with, saved next to it in data/slab2/unit_source_grid.meta.json. Steps 7, 7b and 9 reuse the saved mesh only when the input file asks for the same settings, and rebuild it otherwise. See The mesh.

G

GCMT
The Global Centroid Moment Tensor catalogue: the earthquakes with a moment tensor solution, worldwide, each with its magnitude, location and fault orientation. The package uses the window 1976-01-01 to 2017-03-01. See Earthquake catalogue.
Gutenberg-Richter (GR)
The law that smaller earthquakes are exponentially more frequent than large ones: log10 of the rate equals a minus b times the magnitude. Two shapes of the law are used: truncated and characteristic, with weights of 70 and 30 per cent. See Logic tree and rates.

H

HS
Heterogeneous slip: a scenario whose slip is uneven, concentrated in patches, with the same moment as its FAUS scenario. It is optional (step 7b) and is never used by the rates. See HS and VAUS slip.
hypocentre
The point where an earthquake starts. See Earthquake catalogue.

L

lm (Levenberg-Marquardt)
The least-squares method that rptha uses to place the down-dip lines. It is also the value of --discretizer that runs rptha's mesh exactly, with no repair. See optimal, lm and mid.
local datum
The reference depth of each point: the depth of the trench nearest to it. The contour depths are measured from it, as depth below the nearby trench. See Contours from SLAB.
logic tree
All the combinations of the uncertain inputs, each one a branch with a weight. The rates of the zone are the weighted summary of the branches. See PTHA in plain words.

M

mesh
All the unit sources of a zone, as a grid: columns along strike and rows down dip. See The mesh.
moment balance
The rule that, over a long time, the earthquakes of a zone release the moment the plates accumulate: the area, the rigidity, the slip rate and the coupling together. It fixes the rates of each branch (level 2). See Logic tree and rates.
moment tensor, nodal plane
The description of an earthquake's source in the GCMT catalogue. It gives two possible fault planes, the nodal planes, each with a strike, a dip and a rake. See Earthquake catalogue.
Mw (moment magnitude)
The logarithmic size of an earthquake: Mw = (2/3)(log10 M0 - 9.05), with the moment M0 in newton metres. One unit more means about 32 times more energy. See PTHA in plain words.
Mw max
The largest magnitude a branch allows. It is derived from the largest magnitude observed, and the program caps it at 9.6. See Logic tree and rates.
mw_max_observed
The largest magnitude thought to have occurred on the zone. Step 6 takes the larger of Berryman et al. (2015)'s value for the zone and the largest earthquake of the GCMT subset, and the engine sets the lower end of the range of Mw max from it (the upper end comes from the geometry of the zone). See The input file.

N

NCI
Australia's National Computational Infrastructure, whose public THREDDS server holds PTHA18's published files. Step 8 and the --download-official-hs-vaus flag read from it. See The official PTHA18 run.
NDK file
The text format of the GCMT catalogue files: five lines for every earthquake, with its location, depth, moment tensor and the two nodal planes. Step 4 downloads them into data/gcmt/. See Earthquake catalogue.
NetCDF
A file format for gridded data. The SLAB depth grids (.grd) and PTHA18's unit-source tables (.nc) use it. See Contours from SLAB.

O

optimal
The default --discretizer: rptha's mesh, kept unchanged unless it has a folded, collapsed or overlapping cell, and repaired only then. See optimal, lm and mid.

P

percentile
The value below which a given share of the branch weight lies. The report gives the 2.5th, 16th, 50th (the median), 84th and 97.5th percentiles of the rates. See PTHA in plain words.
plate pair
The two plates that meet along a stretch of a boundary in Bird (2003)'s model, for example the Tonga and Pacific plates. With --segment-boundaries bird, a new segment starts where the pair changes. See Segmented vs unsegmented.
prior weight, posterior weight
The weight of a branch before and after the Bayesian update. Both are columns of logic_tree_branches_ZONE.csv (prior_prob and posterior_prob). See Logic tree and rates.
PTHA18
The 2018 Australian probabilistic tsunami hazard assessment by Geoscience Australia. This package rebuilds its earthquake-source part from public data and checks the result against PTHA18's published files. See Home.

Q

q (rupture-overlap correction)
A correction for ruptures that share cells. With --rupture-size local, each rupture's conditional probability is multiplied by q, so that places covered by many overlapping ruptures do not get a higher rate. See Rupture size.

R

R, Rscript
The statistical language in which rptha and PTHA18 were written. Only step 8 needs it, to read PTHA18's saved session. See The official PTHA18 run.
rake
The direction of slip on the fault plane. A rake of 90 degrees is a pure thrust: the upper plate moves straight up the slope. The scenarios of the package are pure thrusts. See Earthquake catalogue.
ramp (trench end)
A trench end that climbs in depth like a ramp, more than 3 km deeper than the trench's normal depth, is the edge of the SLAB data and not the trench. It is trimmed, by at most 100 km. See Contours from SLAB.
rptha
Geoscience Australia's R package, in which PTHA18 was computed. This package reimplements its methods in Python. Steps 1 to 7 and 9 read no rptha file; step 8 needs R with rptha installed. See Python and rptha, side by side.
rupture size: rptha and local
How many cells a uniform-slip scenario gets. rptha: one block of cells per magnitude, from the zone's mean cell size. local: a block per placement, from the real area of its own cells. See Rupture size.

S

s-matrix
How rptha describes the down-dip lines: for every contour, the fraction of its length at which each line crosses it. See The mesh.
scaling relation (Strasser)
An empirical rule for the length and width of a rupture of a given magnitude. PTHA18 uses the relation of Strasser et al. (2010) for subduction interfaces. See Logic tree and rates.
ScienceBase
The USGS data catalogue that hosts the SLAB2.0 grids. Step 1 downloads a grid from it only when the grid is not on disk. See Troubleshooting.
segment
A stretch of a zone that can break on its own, with its own convergence, coupling and earthquakes. By default a segment is one of the segments of Berryman et al. (2015) Table 3.1; with --segment-boundaries bird it is a Bird (2003) plate pair. See Segmented vs unsegmented.
segmentation (level 0)
Whether a zone breaks as a whole or in separate segments. Both are weighted at 0.5, and the segments are summed within the segmented half. The option is --segmented true; without it every zone is run unsegmented. See Segmented vs unsegmented.
seismic moment (M0)
The physical size of an earthquake: rigidity times area times slip, in newton metres. See PTHA in plain words.
seismogenic cutoff
The depth below which the interface is too hot for large earthquakes. It is the deepest contour of the zone, and its value comes from Berryman et al. (2015). See Contours from SLAB.
SFFM
The stochastic slip generator of Davies et al. (2015), which makes the heterogeneous-slip fields of step 7b. See HS and VAUS slip.
shapefile
A common file format for lines and polygons, made of several files with the same name (.shp, .dbf, .shx, .prj). Step 1 writes the contours as one. See Contours from SLAB.
shear modulus (rigidity, mu)
How stiff the rock is. The moment is the rigidity times the area times the slip. Steps 7 and 7b use one constant value, 30 GPa for every zone the package builds. Step 7c uses a depth curve from 10 GPa near the trench to 30 GPa at 15 km and 67 GPa from 35 km. See Variable shear modulus.
slab
The part of an oceanic plate that has sunk into the mantle. See Contours from SLAB.
SLAB2.0, SLAB1.0
The USGS models of the depth of subducting plates, on map grids. A zone whose name ends in 2 is built from SLAB2.0, and any other zone from SLAB1.0. Step 1 finds the depth grid on disk or downloads it; the SLAB2.0 grids of eight regions ship with the package. See Contours from SLAB.
source zone (level 0 mix)
On a segmented run, the zone's own answer: the unsegmented branch times 0.5, plus the union of the segments times 0.5. It is the last column of the rate curves file. See Segmented vs unsegmented.
strike
The direction of a line on the fault, in degrees clockwise from north, with the plate dipping to the right of it. See The mesh.

T

trench
The long, deep groove of the sea floor where the plate starts to sink. It is the depth-0 contour and the top edge of the mesh. Step 1 finds it as the up-dip edge of the SLAB raster. See Contours from SLAB.
TVD (total variation distance)
How much the Bayesian update moved the branch weights: 0 means not at all and 1 means completely. It is written to logic_tree_summary.csv. See Logic tree and rates.

U

union of segments
The segments added up: the second half of the level 0 mix on a segmented run. It is the column exceedance_rate_union_of_segments of rate_curves.csv. See Segmented vs unsegmented.
unit source
One cell of the mesh, about 50 km across: the building block of every scenario. See The mesh.
unsegmented branch
The zone as one fault. On an unsegmented run it is the whole answer, with weight 1. On a segmented run it is half of the answer, with weight 0.5. It is the column exceedance_rate_unsegmented. See Segmented vs unsegmented.

V

variable shear modulus
PTHA18's second set of rates, with a rigidity that depends on depth. Each scenario's magnitude is relabelled with it, and the difference is treated as an error of the catalogue magnitudes in level 3. See Variable shear modulus.
VAUS
Variable Area Uniform Slip: from a heterogeneous-slip field, the smallest rectangle that contains it, with uniform slip and the same moment. It is optional (step 7b). See HS and VAUS slip.
Definitions follow the steps of the nine-step pipeline; the values quoted are the ones the package uses.
